# How the field actually bifurcates content

**Researched 11 Sep 2026.** What the best-regarded practitioners and agencies use, what evidence sits
behind each model, and which parts survive scrutiny.

**Read the source-quality note first.** It changes how much of the rest you should believe.

---

## 0. Source quality, because this field is almost entirely vendor-written

Searching for content-funnel methodology returns, in order: agencies selling content marketing,
tools selling attribution, and consultants selling frameworks. **Almost every "framework" is a lead
magnet for the company that published it.** That does not make them wrong. It means the incentive
runs toward complexity and novelty rather than toward accuracy, and nothing here should be adopted
because it is well-known.

| Grade | Meaning | What qualified |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Peer-reviewed or large-sample primary research | Gartner buying-journey work; one ScienceDirect paper on startup maturity and digital marketing |
| **B** | Practitioner with a track record and a public method, selling something | Refine Labs, MKT1, April Dunford, Bob Moesta |
| **C** | Agency or tool content marketing | Most of the rest |
| **D** | Listicles, LinkedIn opinion, Medium reposts | The entire first page of results for "TOFU MOFU BOFU" |

**The funnel model everyone uses is grade D.** Not one primary source exists for it. It is folklore
that got repeated until it sounded like fact.

## 1. The four models actually in use

### Model 1: the funnel. TOFU / MOFU / BOFU

**What it says.** Content sorts into awareness, consideration and decision.

**Why it persists.** It is the only model everyone already knows, it maps onto how budgets and teams
are organised, and it gives a content calendar an obvious shape.

**Where it breaks, and this is the operative problem.** It classifies **topics**, and a topic is not
a stage. Treatment is. The same subject is top of funnel when it defines a term and bottom of funnel
when it prices a specific case. Every team that builds this spreadsheet discovers the arbitrariness
at the edges and assumes they built it wrong. They did not; the object is wrong.

**Verdict: keep as an output label, never as the engine.** People need the vocabulary. The
classification should not depend on it.

### Model 2: buying jobs, from Gartner (grade A)

**What it says.** Buyers do a set of jobs, revisited in loops and in parallel across a buying group,
rather than moving through stages: problem identification, solution exploration, requirements
building, supplier selection, validation, consensus creation.

**Why it is better for classification.** A job is something a piece of content either does or does
not do for a reader, which is checkable. And it handles the thing the funnel cannot: **a buying
group where different people are doing different jobs at the same time.** In a committee purchase
that is not an edge case, it is the normal state.

**Two cautions.** Gartner's own numbers vary between publications, 67% versus 75% for rep-free
preference on two of their pages, with no reconciliation offered. And the secondary summaries
consistently call the jobs a "6-stage framework", which is the exact mistranslation that regenerates
the funnel. **Read the primary source, not the summaries.**

### Model 3: demand creation versus demand capture, from Refine Labs (grade B)

**What it says.** Two fundamentally different jobs, not two stages of one. **Demand creation** makes
someone realise they have a problem, and happens where you cannot attribute it: podcasts, LinkedIn,
peer conversation, what Chris Walker calls dark social. **Demand capture** catches people already
looking: search, review sites, comparison pages.

**Why it matters more than the funnel for channel decisions.** It is a claim about *where*, not just
*what*. And it explains the thing that confuses most teams: the channel that produces pipeline is
usually not the channel that gets credit for it, because creation is unattributable by construction.

**This is the best-grounded version of the channel split**, and it maps directly onto the
personal-profile versus company-page question (see `03-channel-split-and-stage.md`).

**Caution:** Refine Labs sells the remedy. The concept stands on its own logic; the effect sizes
they publish should be treated as marketing.

### Model 4: Brand, Demand, Expand, also Refine Labs (grade B)

**What it says.** Three stages that build on each other, where brand is the foundation that makes
demand work harder. Their own framing: paid media works best when buyers already know who you are,
so without brand "you're paying to reach people who don't care yet".

**Why it belongs here.** It is a **maturity and sequencing** model rather than a funnel, and it
answers a question the funnel cannot: *what should a company at this stage do first?* That is
directly relevant to growth-stage companies and to anyone deciding whether to invest in founder
content before performance marketing.

## 2. What the strongest operators have in common

Across the grade-B sources, three things recur, and they matter more than which model anyone uses.

**Original evidence beats opinion.** The agencies with the best reputations run surveys, publish
proprietary benchmarks and original research. The reason is straightforward: opinion is infinitely
reproducible and evidence is not. One agency comparison put it as buyers trusting "original evidence
more than opinions".

**Reusable frameworks, which MKT1 calls Content IP.** Emily Kramer's stated principle is to create
reusable frameworks and avoid vanilla content. **A framework is an asset that compounds; a post is
consumed once.** This is also the honest answer to "how do we make this not generic": generic is
what you get when the output is the content rather than the method.

**The measurement is the moat, not the taxonomy.** Every operator worth reading is arguing about
what to measure and how to attribute it. None of them are arguing about whether TOFU means
awareness. The taxonomy is the cheap part.

## 3. What this means for the engine

1. **Classify on buying jobs. Label as funnel.** Confirmed by ruling 2.
2. **Carry demand creation versus demand capture as a separate axis**, because it governs channel
   choice and the funnel does not. A piece can be creation on LinkedIn and capture on a website with
   the same substance.
3. **Carry company maturity as a profile field**, because Brand-Demand-Expand is right that
   sequencing changes what to do first, and a growth-stage company and a scaled one should get
   different recommendations from the same inputs.
4. **Weight original evidence in Fit.** A piece carrying the company's own data should score above
   one restating industry generalities, because that is what separates citable from ignorable.
5. **Grade every source that enters the system.** The table at the top of this file is the mechanism.
   Most published statistics in this field come from someone selling the remedy.
