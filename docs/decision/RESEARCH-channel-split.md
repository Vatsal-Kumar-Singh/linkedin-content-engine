# The channel split, and when it applies

**Vatsal's hypothesis, 11 Sep 2026:**

> TOFU/MOFU content for personal pages of C-level executives, MOFU/BOFU content for the company
> page. This is specifically for companies who can leverage their founder network and are in the
> growing phase. Even questions like these should be asked by the engine.

**Verdict: the channel half is measured and holds. The funnel-stage half is a reasonable hypothesis
and is not measured.** Both parts are worth keeping, labelled differently. The conditions he attached
are the most valuable part and belong in the intake.

---

## 1. What we measured ourselves

From one industrial-B2B project: 267 original posts across 12 named executives plus a competitor
company-page corpus.

| Finding | Number |
|---|---|
| Personal profile against company page | roughly **5x** engagement |
| Employee post against company-curated content | roughly **9x** |
| Original post against a bare repost of the company | **141 against 67** median likes |
| Indian executives against global executives | **213 against 91** median likes |
| Length, 180+ words against 50 to 99 | **283.5 against 61** |

**The channel asymmetry is real and large.** A company page is structurally incapable of the reach a
named person gets, and the gap is not a content-quality problem. That much is settled.

## 2. Why the funnel half is unproven, and what it probably is instead

The hypothesis says personal pages carry TOFU/MOFU and company pages carry MOFU/BOFU. **We have not
measured any post against a funnel stage**, so this is inference rather than evidence.

**The better-grounded version of the same intuition is demand creation versus demand capture.** A
named person on LinkedIn is doing demand creation: reaching people who are not looking, in a place
where attribution fails by construction. A company page and a website are doing demand capture:
found by people already looking, where the job is to be credible and complete.

That reframing does three useful things:

1. It explains **why** the split works rather than asserting it. The channels differ in who is
   already looking, not in which funnel third they occupy.
2. It survives the objection that a topic is not a stage, because creation and capture are about
   the reader's state, not the subject.
3. It predicts the exception correctly: **a company page post can absolutely do creation** if it
   travels, and an executive post can do capture if someone searches their name after a meeting.

**Proposed for the engine:** carry demand creation versus capture as the channel axis, emit the
funnel label alongside, and mark the funnel mapping as inferred until somebody measures it.

## 3. The conditions are the valuable part

Vatsal attached two, and they are the difference between a rule and a recipe.

**Condition 1: the company has a founder or executive network it can use.** Not every company can.
This fails when the executives have no network in the buying market, will not post, or are not
credible in the category. **An executive-led strategy that depends on an executive who will not
participate is the most common way this fails**, and it fails silently for a quarter before anyone
admits it.

One measured case makes it concrete: a CTO with **four original posts in nine years**, and a median
identical to the ANSCER CTO's. The strategy is sound and the input is the constraint.

**Condition 2: growth phase.** This matches the Brand-Demand-Expand sequencing argument, that brand
is the foundation which makes demand work harder. A growth-stage company usually has no brand
familiarity to trade on, so demand capture catches almost nobody, because few are searching for a
category they do not know exists. **Creation has to come first, and a named human is the cheapest
creation channel there is.**

A scaled company with existing category demand can invert this and run capture-heavy.

## 4. When the split does not apply

Worth writing down, because a framework that only knows its own success case is a recipe.

- **The executives will not or cannot post.** Then the company page is what you have. Do not build a
  plan on an input nobody has agreed to supply.
- **Category demand already exists and you are a known name.** Capture gets cheaper and creation
  gets less urgent.
- **The buyer is not on the platform.** Our own corpus shows Indian executives at 213 median against
  91 global, on the same platform. Channel effects are market-specific and should be measured per
  profile rather than assumed.
- **Regulated speech.** Where a named individual's claim carries personal or regulatory exposure,
  the calculus changes and the company page may be the safer publisher.

## 5. What the engine should ask

These are in the question bank, and they exist because of this section.

1. Who at the company will actually post, under their own name, this quarter?
2. How many original posts have they written in the last year? *(Asked this way on purpose. "Will
   you post?" gets a yes from everyone.)*
3. Does your buyer know this category exists, or are you explaining a new idea?
4. When someone hears about you, where do they go to check you are real?
5. Is anyone searching for what you sell, in the words they would use?
6. Can a named person make a claim about your product without creating exposure?

**Question 2 is the one that predicts failure.** Stated intent is worthless; past posting behaviour
is the only honest input, and it is public.
