# Distribution — what actually moves a post in 2026

The headline finding, because it inverts what most people still do:

> **Posts without hashtags outperform posts with them by 5–10%.**

That is not a style preference. LinkedIn dismantled the machinery hashtags used
to run on: it removed the ability to follow a hashtag (2024), removed hashtag
fields from profiles, and retired Creator Mode hashtag topics. Nothing on the
platform routes content by tag any more.

## What replaced them: Topic Authority

Distribution moved from a **Relationship Graph** (who you know) to an
**Interest Graph** (what you are about). The platform assigns every account a
topic fingerprint, built from three inputs:

| Input | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Topic consistency | Do your posts sit in the same two or three subjects? |
| Engagement quality | Do the right people engage, and for how long? |
| Semantic clarity | Can the post be classified cleanly from its own words? |

An account posting across unrelated subjects asks the algorithm to classify it
as several different accounts, and gets a smaller, less consistent audience each
time. **One well-anchored post outperforms three scattered ones** — the instinct
when reach drops is to post more, and the data points the other way.

### What this means for us specifically

Two things, and both are convenient rather than costly.

**Saying your subject word is a distribution mechanism, not just a copy rule.** Gate
C0.1 already requires it in every headline for readability reasons. It turns out
to be how the post gets classified as well. The editorial argument and the
algorithmic one agree.

**Narrowing to two or three pain points helps reach.** The decision to focus was
made for message-testing reasons. Topic Authority rewards exactly that shape, so
it pays twice.

## Hashtag rules

| Count | Use |
|---|---|
| **0–3** | Default. Restate the subject already in the copy. |
| 4–5 | Events and campaigns only, where community aggregation genuinely matters. |
| **10+** | **30–50% visibility penalty.** Never. |

Hashtags are a lightweight topic signal. A tag should name the thing the post is
already about — `#DataEngineering` on a pipeline post is fine. A tag reaching for a
bigger audience than the post deserves (`#AI`, `#Innovation`,
`#DigitalTransformation`, `#Leadership`) is the tell of an account that does not
know what it is about, which is precisely what the Interest Graph punishes.

## Company page versus personal

Company page organic reach is suppressed roughly 60–66% against personal
profiles, and one widely-repeated 2026 summary puts "post from your personal
profile, not your company page" at the top of its list.

We publish from the company page by decision, so the compensating lever is
colleagues **engaging in the first hour** — not posting, engaging. On a small
page that is worth more than any content variable: modelled at a 400-follower
page, ten colleagues engaging is roughly a 27× difference in reach. See the
strategy note in `kb/linkedin-content-engine.md`.

## Benchmarks

| Measure | Aim |
|---|---|
| Impressions | 1.5–2× follower count |
| Follower growth | +1–3% from a strong carousel |
| Dwell | The Depth Score measures how long people actually read, not clicks |

Dwell is why a carousel outperforms a text post and why an external link in the
post body costs ~60% of reach: a link sends the reader away, and the platform is
counting the seconds they stay.
