# Splitting copy between the image and the caption

They are not the same words twice, and they are not two unrelated pieces either.

## The division of labour

| | Carries | Written for |
|---|---|---|
| **Image** | The argument | Someone who never expands the caption |
| **Caption** | The detail the image cannot hold | Someone the image already stopped |

**The image has to work with the caption collapsed**, because that is how most
people meet it. Cover the caption; if the image no longer means anything, the
argument is in the wrong place.

## The caption opens by mirroring the image

The strongest and most-repeated rule in the carousel research: **mirror the
cover's hook in the first line of the caption**, then go somewhere new.

This is not redundancy. The reader who expands the caption has just read the
image; repeating its hook confirms they are in the right place and gives the
paragraph a running start. What kills a caption is opening with something
unrelated, which reads as two posts stapled together.

Sequence that works:

1. Mirror the hook from the image
2. The detail, the example, or the mechanism the image had no room for
3. One prompt to respond — a question, not a request for a demo
4. Hashtags, 0–3, last

## Length

| Format | Target |
|---|---|
| Carousel caption | 1,200+ characters |
| Text post | 1,301–2,500 characters |
| Cover slide headline | **6–8 words maximum** |
| First line of caption | under 10 words |

## Rules that come from our own defects

**A qualifier lives wherever its claim lives.** If a number is on the creative,
its qualifier is on the creative too. Captions truncate at "see more"; the image
does not. A qualified claim on the image and its qualifier stranded below the
fold is an unqualified claim.

**The CTA goes in the caption, never in the image.** Three reasons and all three
hold: an in-image CTA cannot be clicked, it dates the asset the moment the offer
changes, and on a video post the platform paints its own player chrome across
the bottom strip where a CTA would sit. This is enforced by a test — no card
template may render the `cta` field.

**Do not put the category in the corner.** A small label reading "PIPELINE
OPERATIONS" in a corner does not make the post legible: nobody reads the corner.
The subject belongs in the headline, at the size the headline is set in.

## Carousels specifically

- Completion rates fall after **slide 12**. Longer only if the story earns it.
- Every slide has to earn the swipe — a slide that only restates the previous one
  is where readers stop.
- The cover is the only slide most people see. It carries the same load as a
  single card and should be written to the same standard.
