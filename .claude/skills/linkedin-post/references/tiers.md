# Tiers — TOFU, MOFU, BOFU

## The test that catches most misclassification

> **TOFU = what is · MOFU = how to · BOFU = how WE**

Apply it to a draft in seconds. If a post you have labelled TOFU explains how
*we* do something, it is not TOFU — it is BOFU wearing a TOFU headline, which is
the single most common failure and the one that converts nobody.

## What each tier is actually for

### TOFU — recognition

Names the problem and **stops**. No product, no mechanism, no us. The reader
should finish it thinking "that is my Tuesday", not "I see what they sell".

The reason to hold this line is the 95-5 rule: at any moment roughly 95% of
buyers are not in market. A TOFU post that pitches in its last line spends the
95% to reach the 5%, and the 5% were going to find you anyway.

**Where the value column may appear: nowhere.** This is the rule the calendar
did not enforce, and the one most likely to slip, because every pain point in
`kb/pain-points.md` is written as problem *plus* what we do about it. On TOFU
only the left column ships.

### MOFU — the honest comparison

The reader knows they have the problem and is weighing ways out. Now the value
column appears — but beside the alternatives, not instead of them.

A MOFU post that presents only our answer is TOFU with a pitch bolted on. Name
the real options, including hiring and doing nothing, and be honest about where
each one is actually the right call. Credibility here is what makes BOFU
believable later.

### BOFU — specifics

Scope, price shape, what is excluded, who owns what when it breaks. The buyer is
in market and wants to know whether this fits.

**Naming a limit is the strongest move available at BOFU.** "Which tickets we
answer, and which always reach a person" is more persuasive than any list of
inclusions, because an unbounded claim is what the reader has been disappointed
by before.

## A caveat worth carrying

Practitioners reasonably argue the funnel is a poor fit for LinkedIn organic:
buyers do not move through tidy stages, and the same post reaches all three at
once — someone binges your case studies on Monday and forgets you exist by
Friday.

That critique is right about the *audience* and wrong about the *writing*. The
tiers remain useful as a discipline on what a given post is allowed to do, even
though no reader experiences them in order. Treat them as constraints on the
draft, not as a journey the reader is on.

## Mix

At a small follower count, weight heavily to TOFU — it is the only tier that
reaches people who do not follow you yet.

| Tier | Under ~1,000 followers | At 5,000+ |
|---|---|---|
| TOFU | ~70% | ~50% |
| MOFU | ~20% | ~30% |
| BOFU | ~10%, on demand | ~20% |

**BOFU is not really feed content.** It is sales enablement that happens to live
on LinkedIn: an asset a rep sends in a follow-up, links in a DM, or shows on a
call. Its audience is the three people in your pipeline, not the feed. Judge it
by whether a rep used it, not by impressions — and make it when sales asks,
rather than on a schedule.
